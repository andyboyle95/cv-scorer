Decision one

How big is it, really?

The thing people most often get wrong, and the one thing a spec sheet can't tell you. These are drawn to true physical scale.

Compare
See them at actual size Hold a bank card against your screen and drag until the blue card matches it.
85.60 × 53.98 mm — standard bank card
Why this earns its place. Screen size is quoted as a diagonal measurement of the display, which tells you almost nothing about whether the phone fits your hand or your pocket — and the gap between a 6.3" and a 6.9" iPhone is the single most common source of buyer's regret. Calibrating against a bank card (85.6 mm, identical worldwide) turns an abstract number into something you can physically hold up. No comparison page in UK telco does this.

Show, don't specify. The camera is the main reason to move up a tier, and it's the spec people understand least. Option A — design notes

What changed since the last round

Two constraints reshaped this completely: it uses only official O2 assets, and it carries no pricing anywhere — no monthly cost, no upfront, no links to live deals.

That second constraint is the interesting one. Without price or a route to basket, this cannot be a conversion page. Its only job is to get someone to a confident decision and hand them to the shop. So the design stops being retail furniture and becomes a set of demonstrations.

The core idea

All iPhones are nearly the same. Only five things genuinely differ: size, camera, screen, chip and battery. A table gives all seven columns equal weight and buries that. This page devotes one section to each real decision and shows the difference rather than describing it.

The three things that make it work

  • True-scale sizing with bank-card calibration. A 6.9-inch diagonal is meaningless; a phone you can hold your card against is not. Size is the most common regret and the least well served online.
  • The zoom demo. Camera is the main reason to move up a tier and the least understood spec. Two phones, the same shot from row Z at The O2, with honest degradation past each one's optical limit.
  • Comparison arrives last. You build a shortlist while learning what the differences mean, and the final view fades out everything the models share.

Assets

Everything is official and embedded, so the file works offline:

  • Product photography for the five iPhone 17 models, and family shots for the 16 and 15 ranges — O2's Storyblok CDN.
  • The O2 logo, as inline SVG so it takes the brand blue.
  • On Air, O2's own variable brand typeface.
  • Colour tokens read from o2.co.uk's stylesheet: #0050ff primary, #00008c hover, #0019a5 footer navy, #d4e2ff tint.
Asset gap. O2's shop pages sit behind a Queue-it waiting room, so only the iPhone 17 family has individually retrievable cut-outs. The 16 and 15 models use official family photography instead, which is why they're presented as groups. That grouping is the right IA anyway — but nine individual product shots would need pulling from the DAM before build.

Launch readiness

A new model is one object in the data file. It appears in the top tier, in all four decision tools, in the shortlist comparison and in the full table. Launch day is a content change, not a build. A pre-announcement model can sit in the top tier with empty spec values and a register-interest link.

How you'd measure it

Primary: click-through into the shop, and assisted device sales.
Secondary: tool engagement (size calibrations, zoom steps, shortlists built), scroll depth on mobile, exit rate to apple.com.
Guardrail: organic entrances for "iPhone comparison" — the full spec table stays on the page for exactly this reason.

Effort

Bigger than Option B: roughly 8–10 weeks with a designer, a front-end developer and a content designer. The four tools are independent, so they can ship one at a time — size first, since it needs only dimensions data.

Content issues found in the live table

Worth fixing whichever direction you take:
  • iPhone 16 Plus is listed as 6.1-inch. It's 6.7-inch — and the bigger screen is the only reason to choose it over the 16.
  • iPhone 15 and 15 Plus are credited with a "12MP Telephoto with 2X Optical Zoom". Neither has a telephoto lens; the 2× is a crop of the main sensor.
  • iPhone 15 Pro says "A17 Bionic" while the 15 Pro Max says "A17 Pro". Both are A17 Pro.
  • iPhone 16 Pro Max lists "48MP Macro" as a fourth camera. Macro is a mode on the ultra-wide.
  • iPhone 17 Pro's camera cell says "48MP Fusion camera system" twice.
  • Optical zoom figures are given for the 15 and 16 ranges but omitted for the 17 Pro models — the newest and most capable cameras on the page.
  • Row order (17, Air, 17 Pro, 17 Pro Max, 17e, 16e, 16, 16 Plus…) follows neither tier nor generation nor the order of the editorial section above it.
