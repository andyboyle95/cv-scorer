Common questions

Still deciding?

Which iPhone has the best camera?
iPhone 17 Pro Max and iPhone 17 Pro share the same triple 48MP Pro Fusion system — Main, Ultra Wide and Telephoto all at 48MP. If you mostly shoot everyday photos rather than zoomed shots, the dual 48MP system on iPhone 17 gets you most of the way for less.
Which iPhone has the longest battery life?
iPhone 17 Pro Max, at up to 37 hours of video playback — the longest of any iPhone. iPhone 17 is next at 30 hours, which beats every iPhone 16 model except the 16 Pro Max.
Is it worth upgrading from an iPhone 15?
From a standard iPhone 15 to an iPhone 17 you gain a 120Hz ProMotion always-on display, a second 48MP camera, the A19 chip with Apple Intelligence, and 50% more battery. From a 15 Pro the jump is smaller — compare them side by side above with "differences only" switched on.
What's the difference between iPhone 17 and iPhone Air?
iPhone Air is the thinnest iPhone ever made and has a bigger 6.5-inch screen and the faster A19 Pro chip. iPhone 17 has an extra rear camera and three more hours of battery. Choose Air for the design, 17 for the camera.
What's the cheapest iPhone with Apple Intelligence?
iPhone 16e. Every iPhone 16 and 17 model supports Apple Intelligence, along with iPhone 15 Pro and 15 Pro Max.
Can I change my mind after I order?
With O2 Switch Up you can upgrade to a new phone after just three months, without paying off the rest of your device plan.

Option A — design notes

The problem

Today the page is a wall of prose followed by a 14 × 7 spec table, with no images, no prices, no filtering, no CTAs in the comparison and no way to shortlist. It's a datasheet on a retail site: it describes the range but never helps anyone choose, and it gives the visitor nowhere to buy.

The shape of the new page

  • Launch strip — a permanent four-state component (Rumoured → Announced → Pre-order → On sale) so launch week is a content toggle, not a build.
  • Guided finder — three questions, three ranked recommendations, each with the reasons it was picked.
  • Filterable card grid — picture, price, "best for", key specs, CTA, compare tick.
  • Compare tray and drawer — shortlist while you browse, then compare two or three properly, with "differences only" and best-in-row highlighting.
  • Trade-in estimator — mid-decision, where it changes the answer.
  • Signed-in upgrade slot — turn "From £x" into "£y for you".
  • FAQ block — answers the questions this page already ranks for, marked up as FAQPage schema.

What it's designed to move

  • Click-through to PDP. Today there is no route from the comparison to a product. Now every card and every compare column has one.
  • Fewer exits to apple.com. Visitors leave to find prices and pictures. Both are now here.
  • Mobile completion. The current table is a horizontal-scroll block on the majority of sessions. Cards and a two-column drawer are built mobile-first.
  • Organic traffic. The FAQ and per-model "best for" lines give search engines something to lift.
  • Intent data. Finder answers and compare pairings tell merchandising what people are actually weighing up.

Suggested measurement

Primary: comparison → PDP click-through rate, and assisted device conversion.
Secondary: finder completion rate, compares started per session, mobile scroll depth, exit rate to apple.com.
Guardrail: organic entrances and rankings for "iPhone comparison".

Phasing

Phase 1 — ship Option B's compare module (cards + tray + drawer). It is the same component, so nothing is thrown away.
Phase 2 — launch strip, filters, FAQ, trade-in.
Phase 3 — guided finder and signed-in personalisation, which need product and CRM data.

Rough effort: 6–8 weeks with a designer, a front-end dev and a content designer, plus pricing-service and My O2 integration for Phase 3. Option B is the first three weeks of it.

Launch readiness

A new model is a data object plus a new flag. It appears in the grid, the finder's ranking, the compare drawer and the full spec list automatically. Pre-announcement it renders as a "Coming soon" card with a register-interest CTA and empty spec values — the card is in the grid on this page now, as a demonstration.

Content issues found in the live table

Worth fixing whichever option you pick:
  • iPhone 16 Plus listed as 6.1-inch. It is 6.7-inch — the only reason to choose it over the 16.
  • iPhone 15 and 15 Plus credited with a "12MP Telephoto with 2X Optical Zoom". Neither has a telephoto lens.
  • iPhone 15 Pro listed with "A17 Bionic" while 15 Pro Max says "A17 Pro". Both are A17 Pro.
  • iPhone 16 Pro Max lists "48MP Macro" as a fourth camera. Macro is a mode on the ultra-wide.
  • iPhone 17 Pro's camera cell repeats "48MP Fusion camera system" twice.
  • Row order matches neither price, tier, nor the editorial section above it.
