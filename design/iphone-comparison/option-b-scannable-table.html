Option B — design notes

The brief

Change the table and nothing else. No new page template, no IA work, no copy sign-off, no nav changes. One component, one sprint.

The diagnosis

A table's job is to let you compare down a column. This one can't, because every cell is a sentence. "A19 Chip with 5 core GPU and 6 core CPU with 16-core Neural Engine" cannot be visually compared with "A18 Pro Chip with 6 core GPU…" — you have to read both and hold them in memory. Fourteen times, across seven columns.

And it doesn't sort. The most common questions people bring to this page — which has the best camera, which lasts longest, which is smallest — are all sorts, and none of them is possible.

The fix, in two moves

  • Encode, don't describe. Screen size becomes a bar. Cameras become filled circles. ProMotion, Always-On and Apple Intelligence become yes/no pills. Battery and chip become gauges. Now a column can be read in one pass instead of fourteen.
  • Make every column sortable. One click answers "which lasts longest" — the thing the page is most often asked and least able to do.

On top of those: filter by generation, tick two or three rows to fade everything they share, expand any row for the original prose, and a mobile card view carrying the same marks rather than a sideways-scrolling table.

Nothing is lost

Every word of the current table is still on the page — in the row expanders and in the full table underneath. The SEO content, the accessibility fallback and the spec-hunter's use case all survive intact. That is what makes this a low-risk change.

No pricing

Deliberate, per the brief: this page can't link to live deals, so it carries no monthly cost, no upfront cost and no tariff information. The three cards at the foot hand off to the shop, My O2 and the wider range instead.

Assets

  • Official O2 product photography for the five iPhone 17 models.
  • The O2 logo as inline SVG, and On Air, O2's own variable brand typeface.
  • Colour tokens read from o2.co.uk's stylesheet: #0050ff, #00008c, #0019a5, #d4e2ff.
Asset gap. O2's shop pages sit behind a Queue-it waiting room, so only the iPhone 17 family has individually retrievable cut-outs. The other nine rows use a neutral outline at the correct aspect ratio; real shots would come from the DAM before build.

Effort and risk

About one sprint for one front-end developer, plus a short content pass to add dimensions and confirm the yes/no values. No CMS work, no new template, no dependency on pricing or My O2 services.

How this differs from Option A

Option A rebuilds the page around four demonstrations and treats comparison as the last step. This keeps the page exactly as it is and fixes the one component people complain about. If you want both, this is a clean first phase — the marks and the model data carry straight over.

Content issues found in the live table

Worth fixing whichever direction you take:
  • iPhone 16 Plus is listed as 6.1-inch. It's 6.7-inch — the only reason to choose it over the 16.
  • iPhone 15 and 15 Plus are credited with a "12MP Telephoto with 2X Optical Zoom". Neither has a telephoto lens.
  • iPhone 15 Pro says "A17 Bionic" while the 15 Pro Max says "A17 Pro". Both are A17 Pro.
  • iPhone 16 Pro Max lists "48MP Macro" as a fourth camera. Macro is a mode on the ultra-wide.
  • iPhone 17 Pro's camera cell says "48MP Fusion camera system" twice.
  • Optical zoom figures are given for the 15 and 16 ranges but omitted for the 17 Pro models — the newest and best cameras on the page.
  • Row order follows neither tier nor generation nor the editorial section above it.
